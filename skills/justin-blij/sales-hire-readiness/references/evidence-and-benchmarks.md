# Evidence and benchmarks

Every figure carries its source, year and population. Quote them that way on the card and never round them up. Each is graded:

- **[A] Primary data:** a named survey or dataset with a sample.
- **[B] Operator rule or self-reported data:** a judgment from someone who did it at scale, or a large crowdsourced dataset. Strong, but not a controlled study.
- **[C] Agency or recruiter range:** use as a range only, never as a single number.

Benchmarks age. Re-check every figure here once a year and replace it when a newer edition of the same source exists.

## The market a first sales hire walks into [A]

The Bridge Group, AE Models, Motions and Metrics, 2026 edition, 158 B2B companies:

| Metric | Finding | What it means for the founder |
|---|---|---|
| AEs at annual quota | 48% (51% in 2024; mid-60s in earlier editions) | A rep missing quota is now normal. One miss says little about the rep. |
| Average ramp | 6.2 months, the longest in the study's history | Half a year of pay before a rep is fully productive. |
| Experience at hire | 3.7 years, up from 2.7 in 2022 | Companies selling larger deals now hire more experienced AEs. |
| Median AE OTE | US$200k | Often the most expensive person in a seed company. |
| Median quota | US$960k, 4.6x OTE | The quota the market expects. Test it against the founder's own history. |
| AI use | Top third of reps by AI use: 57% at quota. Bottom third: 39% | Observational, not causal. Worth asking every candidate about. |

The Bridge Group, SDR Models, Motions and Metrics, 2025: 60% of SDRs at quota, median OTE US$80k, ramp 3.0 months.

RepVue, self-reported, United States, October 2026: 41.7% of AEs at quota. Lower than Bridge Group. Either way, fewer than half.

## How long $1M ARR really takes [A]

ChartMogul SaaS Growth Reports:

- 2023: of companies that reached $1M ARR, the median took 2 years 9 months from first revenue. Best in class took 9 months.
- 2025: 13.4% of tracked companies reached $1M within 3 years, 25.1% within 5 years.

A rep does not shortcut that timeline. A proven motion does.

## Sales leader tenure and turnover [A/B]

- SBI: the average sales leader tenure is about 19 months.
- Gong analysis (2018): VP of Sales tenure fell from 26 to 19 months while the share of reps at quota fell from 63% to 50%.
- Cespedes and Weinfurter, Harvard Business Review (2015): average annual sales turnover of 25% to 30%.

A VP of Sales hired before a motion exists spends most of an average tenure building what the founder should have built.

## Operator rules [B]

**Mark Leslie and Charles Holloway, "The Sales Learning Curve", Harvard Business Review (2006).** Startups scale sales before they are ready. Start with a small group of sellers comfortable with ambiguity who work with product, and only hire faster once each seller makes a positive marginal contribution. Their line: break even comes a little later, but the cash needed to reach it is much smaller.

**Mark Roberge (first sales hire and later CRO at a public SaaS company; now a venture investor).** Product-market fit is a retention fact, not a sales fact. Selling while customers quietly churn is "an infomercial". Define fit as a leading indicator: P% of customers reach value event E within T days. Scale two sellers a month, not ten at once, and accelerate only if the leading indicators hold. Coachability, curiosity, prior success, intelligence and work ethic predicted success. Aggression and objection handling predicted it worst.

**Jason Lemkin (SaaStr).** Close 10 to 20 customers yourself first. Hire people who have sold at your price point and worked at a startup. Hire two reps, not one, or you will not know why it worked or failed. Wait for a VP of Sales until two reps hit quota: a VP accelerates a flywheel, they do not start it. Below about $1M ARR, most startups lack the roughly 50 qualified leads a month needed to keep one rep busy.

**Pete Kazanjy, "Founding Sales".** The founder sells until results are statistically meaningful: 10 to 30 customers, converting first meetings to customers at about 15% to 25%, reliably. Then hire two early-stage "pioneer" sellers, not a VP. Before blaming a rep, check for a deck, a written discovery question list and a demo script. Judge on leading indicators month by month: onboarding in month 1, about 10 to 20 first meetings with about half reaching a second meeting in month 2, proposals in month 3.

## Pipeline building as a first hire [A/B]

Bloomberry analysis of 1,000 postings: GTM engineering job postings up 205% year on year (January to September 2025 against 2024). If the bottleneck is first meetings rather than closing, the first hire may build the pipeline engine before anyone closes from it.

## US pay benchmarks for runway math

Use these only when the founder has no pay number of their own. Show the source on the card.

| Role | Base | OTE | Source, grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDR | US$60k | US$80k to 85k | RepVue 2026 [B]; Bridge Group 2025 [A] |
| AE, all segments | US$100k to 104k | US$200k | RepVue 2026 [B]; Bridge Group 2026 [A] |
| AE, SMB | US$70k to 75k | US$135k to 140k | RepVue 2026 [B] |
| AE, mid-market | US$95k | US$180k | RepVue 2026 [B] |
| AE, enterprise | US$140k | US$275k to 280k | RepVue 2026 [B] |
| Sales manager (closest match to a player-coach) | US$150k | US$275k | RepVue 2026 [B] |
| VP of Sales, all company sizes | US$200k | US$380k | RepVue 2026 [B] |
| VP of Sales, growth stage | n/a | US$350k to 450k | Pavilion GTM Compensation Benchmarks 2025, 1,200 GTM leaders [B] |
| GTM engineer | about US$130k | mostly salary | Bloomberry; State of GTM Engineering 2026 [B] |

Founding AE ranges, seed to Series A [C, recruiter ranges]:

| Segment and stage | Base | OTE | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMB, pre-seed to seed | US$90k to 110k | US$160k to 200k | 0.15% to 0.35% |
| Mid-market, seed to Series A | US$110k to 135k | US$200k to 240k | 0.25% to 0.6% |
| Enterprise, Series A | US$135k to 175k | US$260k to 350k | 0.4% to 1.0% |

Recruiter sources recommend a heavier base for the first sellers (60/40 or 70/30 rather than 50/50), because early months go on building the playbook, not only closing. Outside the US, use local data. As one illustration, Australian founding AE ranges for US-backed scale-ups sit around A$190k to A$230k OTE [C].

Carta: a company's first employee receives a median of about 1% to 1.5% equity over four years, falling fast for later hires. A first seller rarely receives the first-hire grant.

## The VP of Sales comparison, worked

Over nine months (ramp plus a three-month evaluation), at the medians above, before employer costs:

- One VP of Sales at US$380k OTE: about US$285k. One reading.
- Two mid-market pioneers at US$180k OTE, commission guaranteed through ramp: about US$270k. Two readings.
- Two mid-market pioneers on base only: about US$143k.

Against a median seed round of about US$4.1M (Carta, 2025 rounds), two pioneers through evaluation cost roughly 3.5% to 7% of the round in cash pay.

## Statistics the skill must never use [C]

These circulate in founder content with no traceable study. If a founder quotes one, say it has no source.

- "67% of VP Sales fail in their first 18 months."
- "50% to 70% of first sales hires fail."
- "93% of founders regret one of their first five hires."
- "Founders close at 30% to 50% versus 20% for reps."
- "One GTM engineer replaces three to five SDRs."
