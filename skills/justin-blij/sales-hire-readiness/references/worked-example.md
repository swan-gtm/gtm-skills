# Worked example: the founder who wanted a VP of Sales

Fictional company. Tallyroom sells month-end close software to finance teams at mid-sized companies in the US. It raised a seed round and has about $4.1M in the bank. The CEO writes: "The board thinks we need a VP of Sales. I'm spending half my week on sales calls. Can we hire one?"

## Step 1. The pulls

- `{{CRM}}`: 25 closed-won deals in 15 months. 24 owned by the CEO, 1 by the CTO. 91 closed-lost. First meeting dates logged on every deal for the last nine months.
- `{{BILLING}}`: 25 customers, average annual contract $38k, 2 churned at first renewal.
- `{{PRODUCT_ANALYTICS}}`: the event that predicts renewal is the first month-end close completed in the product.
- `{{DOCS}}`: sales folder with deck, demo script, discovery questions, objection notes, ICP page, and a loss review written last month.
- From the CEO: burn $210k a month, no pay number yet, load factor 1.2, can give 8 hours a week to coaching.

## Step 2. The gates

**Gate 1. Retention: Pass.** Share of new customers completing a first month-end close within 45 days, last three quarterly cohorts: 71%, 74%, 76%. Rising. Logo retention at first renewal 92% (23 of 25).

**Gate 2. Founder repeatability: Pass.** 24 founder-closed deals, inside the 10 to 30 range. First-meeting-to-close by quarter: 19%, 23%, 21%. Within four points, inside the 15% to 25% band. Median cycle 54 days, middle half between 41 and 68 days.

**Gate 3. Written motion: Pass.** All six items present. The loss review covers the last ten losses in the buyers' words. Top reason: "no budget until next fiscal year", which also tells a new rep when to call back.

**Gate 4. Pipeline supply: Pass.** First meetings a month, six-month average: 34 (inbound 15, founder network 7, founder outbound 12). The CEO keeps the 8 largest a month, leaving 26.

Need for two reps, from Tallyroom's own numbers, with a quota of $760k (4.2x a $180k OTE, slightly under the market's 4.6x because it is a first seat):

```
deals a year per rep        = 760,000 / 38,000           = 20
first meetings per rep/year = 20 / 0.21                  = about 95
per rep per month           = 95 / 12                    = about 8
needed for two reps         = 16 a month  (26 available)
```

**Gate 5. Runway: Pass.** No pay number from the CEO, so the card uses the US mid-market AE median ($95k base, $180k OTE) and says so. Window: 6 months ramp plus 3 months evaluation.

```
floor   = 2 x 95,000  x 0.75 x 1.2 = $171,000
ceiling = 2 x 180,000 x 0.75 x 1.2 = $324,000
new monthly burn  = 210,000 + 324,000 / 9 = $246,000
months left after = 4,100,000 / 246,000 - 9 = about 7.7   (buffer: 6)
```

**Gate 6. Founder capacity: Low risk.** 8 hours a week across two hires, at the 4-hour default each.

## Step 3. Verdict and profile

All hard gates pass, pipeline covers two reps, and the CEO is still the best seller in the company. Verdict: **Hire two pioneers.**

Average deal $38k is above the $25k line, so the profile is experienced AEs, not earlier-career sellers: closed deals between about $19k and $76k with finance buyers, at companies without a well-known brand, with at least a third of their closed revenue self-sourced.

**Why not a VP of Sales.** At the $380k median OTE, nine months of one VP costs about $342k with Tallyroom's load factor. Two pioneers at the ceiling cost $324k. About the same money, one reading instead of two, and a leader with no team or motion of their own to scale yet. The path to a leader is on the card: when both reps close within a few points of the CEO's 21%, re-run for a player-coach.

## Step 4. The 90-day test

From the CEO's funnel: 58% of first meetings reach a second meeting, 60% of second meetings reach a proposal.

- Month 1: shadow 10 of the CEO's calls, pass the pitch and demo with the CEO, book month-2 meetings.
- Month 2: at least 6 first meetings each (75% of 8), at least 43% to a second meeting (15 points under 58%).
- Month 3: at least 2 proposals each and one late-stage deal.
- Kill or keep: day 90 from start.

## What the run changed

The CEO came in asking for one senior hire and leaves with two mid-level ones, a quota built from their own history instead of a template, and a date by which they will know. The board gets a reason it can check: the cost comparison and the bar that unlocks a sales leader.

## The same company, a different answer

Had Tallyroom shown 6 first meetings a month with no inbound, Gate 4 would have failed and the verdict would have been **Build pipeline first**: one pipeline-building hire measured on closing the gap from 6 to the 24 a month needed (16 for two reps plus the CEO's 8), and a re-run once supply held for two months.
