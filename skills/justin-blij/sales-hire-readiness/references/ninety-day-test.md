# The 90-day leading-indicator test

Bookings cannot judge a rep before ramp ends, and ramp averages about six months. Leading indicators can judge them at month three. Kazanjy's point: had anyone watched the leading indicators, they would have known two months in.

Agree the test with both hires before their start date, in writing. The month-three conversation is then about numbers both sides signed, not about feelings.

## Setting the targets

Start from the founder's own funnel over the last six months. Never from a market benchmark: a benchmark describes someone else's product.

| Founder number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| First meetings a month the founder ran | `{{CRM}}` |
| First meeting to second meeting rate | `{{CRM}}` stage dates |
| Second meeting to proposal rate | `{{CRM}}` |
| Median days from first meeting to proposal | `{{CRM}}` |

Then set each hire's targets as a share of the founder's numbers. Practice defaults:

| Month | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Onboarding | Sits in on at least 10 of the founder's calls. Delivers the pitch and the demo to the founder and passes. Can recite the ICP and the top five objections with answers. Books first meetings for month 2. |
| **2** | First meetings | Runs at least 75% of the ramped per-rep first meetings from the pipeline gate (Kazanjy's range for this month: about 10 to 20). Converts to second meetings within 15 points of the founder's rate (his reference: about half). |
| **3** | Proposals | Has proposals out at a count consistent with the founder's second-meeting-to-proposal rate applied to the hire's own second meetings. At least one deal in late stage. |

For a pipeline-building hire, replace the table with qualified first meetings booked for the founder: month 1 systems and lists live, month 2 at least half the monthly gap on the card closed, month 3 the full gap closed or a dated path to it.

## The card line

```
90-day test: M1 certified on pitch and demo, 10 calls shadowed | M2 [n] first meetings, [x%] to second |
             M3 [n] proposals, 1 late-stage deal | Kill/keep by [date, end of month 3]
```

## Reading the result

Two hires make the test readable. Compare each with the targets, with each other and with the founder.

| Pattern | What it usually means | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| Both on target | The motion transfers | Keep both. Re-test at month six on early bookings |
| One on target, one well short | Probably the person | Coach the short one for 30 days on the specific gap, then decide |
| Both well short on the same metric | Probably the motion or the handover | Fix that stage of the motion before judging either person |
| Both short on first meetings only | Pipeline, not people | Check pipeline supply again. The verdict may have needed a pipeline builder first |
| Both hit meetings, both stall at proposals | Pricing, packaging or ICP drift | Review lost deals with the founder before changing people |

"Well short" by default means under two thirds of target.

## The month-three conversation

Run it with each hire separately, then the founder alone.

1. Show the agreed targets and the actuals side by side. No surprises: the hire has seen the numbers every week.
2. Ask the hire what they think caused any gap before offering a view.
3. Decide one of three: keep, coach for 30 days on one named gap with a new target, or exit.
4. Record the decision and the reason with the card.

Founders fire too late far more often than too early. If both the numbers and the hire's explanation point to the person, a 30-day extension without a named gap is a delay, not a decision.
