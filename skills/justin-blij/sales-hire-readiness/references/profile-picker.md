# Profile picker

The verdict decides who to hire. The profile line on the card must be specific enough that an interviewer can reject a strong CV that does not fit it.

## Verdict 1. Not yet

No hire. The card names the one gap that failed, how to measure it, and the re-run date. If the founder insists on hiring anyway, the only defensible hire is a discovery role: one person, explicitly not on a quota, measured on what they learn and write down, with an agreed date to convert to a quota or end. The card labels it as a discovery hire so nobody later judges it on bookings.

## Verdict 2. Hire two pioneers

**When.** All hard gates pass, pipeline covers two ramped reps, and the founder is still the best seller in the company.

**The profile.**

| Trait | Bar | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Price point | Has closed deals within about 2x of your average deal value, either way | A $15k seller and a $150k seller run different jobs |
| Stage | Has sold at a company without a known brand, a large enablement team or a full pipeline | Big-company sellers rarely survive without the infrastructure |
| Self-sourced pipeline | Built a real share of their own pipeline, with numbers | Your pipeline will not be full on day one |
| Coachability | Changed how they sold after feedback, with a specific example | Roberge's strongest predictor |
| Curiosity | Asks more about your customers than about the comp plan | The first sellers feed product and the founder |

**Why two.** With one rep, a miss is noise when fewer than half of reps in the market hit quota. With two, you can compare them with each other and with the founder.

**Why "pioneers".** Kazanjy's word for sellers who are comfortable building as they sell: the first case study, the first objection answer, the first pricing exception. They are not "settlers" who need a finished playbook.

**Pay.** Use the segment table in `references/evidence-and-benchmarks.md`. Weight the base more heavily than the market's 50/50 (60/40 or 70/30), or guarantee commission through the first months of ramp. Pioneers spend early months building, and a thin base filters out the candidates who can afford to take the risk.

## Verdict 3. Hire a player-coach

**When.** Two or more sellers other than the founder already close at close to the founder's rate. Lemkin's bar: two reps hitting quota. Kazanjy's example: founder at 25%, two reps at about 20% each.

**The profile.** A leader who still carries a number, has hired and coached a small team in a company of similar size, and can show the motion they scaled, not the brand they worked under. They take over coaching and hiring from the founder, and the founder moves back to product and the largest deals.

**Pay.** Between the sales manager and VP of Sales rows in the benchmarks, with part of the variable tied to team attainment.

## Verdict 4. Build pipeline first

**When.** Retention, repeatability, the written motion and runway pass, but first meetings fall short of what two reps need.

**The profile.** One person who builds the pipeline engine before anyone closes from it: list building and enrichment, signal tracking, outbound sequences and the systems behind them. Job titles vary (GTM engineer, growth or outbound lead, founding SDR with systems skills). Judge the profile, not the title:

- Has built an outbound or signal-based pipeline from nothing, and can show the meeting numbers.
- Builds the systems themselves rather than asking for tools.
- Measured on qualified first meetings a month for the founder, with a target that closes the gap on the card.

**Re-run.** When first meetings reach the two-rep level for two consecutive months, re-run the skill. It should then return Hire two pioneers.

## Junior pioneers or experienced AEs

The sources disagree. Kazanjy favours earlier-career pioneers. The Bridge Group's 2026 data shows companies selling larger deals now hire AEs with 3.7 years of experience on average, up from 2.7, and finds the junior AE fading at higher deal sizes. Resolve it with two facts, not a preference:

| | Founder coaches 4+ hours a week | Founder coaches less |
|---|---|---|
| **Average deal under about $25k** | Earlier-career pioneers can work | Experienced AEs |
| **Average deal over about $25k** | Experienced AEs | Experienced AEs, and raise the founder capacity risk |

The deal-size line is a practice default. Move it if your own sellers prove otherwise.

## Why a VP of Sales is never the first hire

Say this on the card, with the numbers:

1. A VP's job is to accelerate a flywheel, not start one (Lemkin). With no motion, they spend their tenure doing the founder's job.
2. Sales leaders last about 19 months on average. Hired before the motion exists, much of that goes on building it.
3. Over nine months, one VP at the median OTE costs about the same as two mid-market pioneers with guaranteed commission, and gives one reading instead of two.
4. The VPs who scaled well-known companies are, in Kazanjy's phrase, "professionally rich" and have not sold in a while. The first seller has to sell.

When a founder wants a VP for the board or for fundraising credibility, offer the player-coach path as the step after two pioneers succeed, with the bar that unlocks it.

## Questions that test the profile

| Trait | Question | A strong answer |
|---|---|---|
| Price point | "Walk me through your three largest deals: value, buyers, length." | Numbers within about 2x of yours, without prompting |
| Stage | "What did you have to build yourself in your last role that a bigger company would have handed you?" | Specific assets they made: a sequence, a deck, a pricing sheet |
| Self-sourced pipeline | "What share of your closed revenue came from meetings you booked yourself?" | A percentage and how they know it |
| Coachability | "Tell me about feedback that changed how you sell." | A specific before and after, ideally with a result |
| Curiosity | "What would you want to know about our customers before your first call?" | Questions about the buyer's problem before the comp plan |
