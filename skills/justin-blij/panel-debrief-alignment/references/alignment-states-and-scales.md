# Alignment states and interest scales

## The four states

Every row on the map gets exactly one state. The state is decided by the evidence, never by how confident anyone sounded.

| State | Meaning | Evidence bar | Action priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BLIND** | One side raised something the other has not registered at all | A quote or behaviour from the side that raised it, AND confirmation the other side never mentioned it when asked | First. Always. |
| **DIVERGENT** | Both sides engaged with the same thing and read it differently | A quote or behaviour from each side, pointing in different directions | Second. This is the workable zone. |
| **UNCLEAR** | Someone has a feeling but no evidence | A comment with no example behind it ("something felt off") | Convert to a question before the next step. Never decide on it. |
| **ALIGNED** | Both sides read the same thing the same way | A quote or behaviour from each side that agrees | Note and move on. |

### Rules for classifying

- **Panel versus panel counts too.** Two interviewers who read the same answer differently is a DIVERGENT row, even before the candidate is involved.
- **BLIND beats DIVERGENT on ties.** If a row could be either, and the candidate has shown no awareness of the concern, it is BLIND.
- **UNCLEAR is honest, not weak.** A gut feel from an experienced interviewer is worth chasing. It is not worth deciding on. The move is always to turn it into a specific question.
- **Contaminated rows.** Feedback given only in a group discussion, after a senior voice spoke, is tagged `(group)` on the map and cannot on its own make a row ALIGNED.

### Examples

- Interviewer: "They never mentioned a number without me asking." Candidate: "I think I made a strong case on results." **BLIND.** The candidate thinks the results landed. The panel thinks they were withheld.
- Interviewer A: "Great at discovery, asked five layers deep." Interviewer B: "Talked over me in the role play." **DIVERGENT.** Same session, two reads. Likely both true: strong questioning, weak listening under pressure.
- Interviewer: "Not sure they'd fit the team." No example. **UNCLEAR.** The next move is "What would you have needed to see to be sure?"

## Panel conviction scale

Rate the panel as a whole, then note any single interviewer sitting two or more points away from the rest. That outlier is usually the most important row on the map.

| Score | What it sounds like | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | "I want them. When can they start? I'd be annoyed if we lost them." | Hire. Move today. |
| 4 | Specific praise, names one real concern, wants to see them again | Strong. Broker the concern and move. |
| 3 | "Solid. Good. Could work." Some specifics, no urgency | Undecided. Needs new evidence, not more time. |
| 2 | Praise with no examples. "Let's see who else is out there." | Slow no. Name it and force it. |
| 1 | Clear specific objection, or refuses to rate | No. Close the loop respectfully and quickly. |

**The disappointment test.** When the panel sits at 3, ask each interviewer: "Would you be disappointed if this person accepted another offer this week?" Anyone who hesitates is not a yes.

## Candidate intent scale

| Score | What it sounds like | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Asks about start dates, the team, the first 90 days. Tells you where you rank. | Committed. Protect against a counter-offer. |
| 4 | Specific enthusiasm tied to the role, names one open question | Keen. Answer the question fast. |
| 3 | "Really interesting, I'd like to learn more." Asks about package first | Open, not sold. Find the real motive. |
| 2 | Slow replies, vague on timing, mentions other processes without detail | Drifting. Ask directly where you stand against the others. |
| 1 | Reschedules, goes quiet, or says they are "keeping options open" | Gone or going. Ask once, directly, then release. |

Ratings are always supported by a quote. A score without a quote is removed from the card.
