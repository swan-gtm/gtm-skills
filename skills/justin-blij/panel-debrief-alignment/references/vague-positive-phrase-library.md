# Vague positive phrase library

The rule: **vague positive equals slow no.** Praise with no specific example, no next step and no urgency is a decline that has not been said out loud yet. Name it, then ask the forcing question. Forcing questions are specific and low drama. They make it easy to give a real answer.

These are the defaults. Add to the library every time a phrase turns out to mean something else in your team, because every team has its own polite no.

## From the panel

| They say | It usually means | Forcing question |
|---|---|---|
| "They were fine." | Not a hire. Fine is not a standard anyone recruits to. | "If they were the only candidate, would you hire them?" |
| "Solid. Good background." | Impressed by the CV, not by the interview. | "What did they say or do in the room that you'd want the team to copy?" |
| "Let's see a couple more." | No conviction, and hoping someone better turns up. | "What would the next candidate need to show that this one didn't?" |
| "Could work with some coaching." | A real gap they have not named. | "Which gap, and how long before it costs us a deal?" |
| "Great culture fit." | Liked them, has not assessed the work. Also a bias risk. | "What did they show you about how they'd do the job?" |
| "Not sure they're senior enough." | Often a style read, not a capability read. | "What's one thing a senior hire would have said that they didn't?" |
| "Happy to go with the group." | Has a view and does not want to own it. | "What would make you a strong yes? What would make you a no?" |
| Silence from one interviewer | The most important read on the panel. | Ask them first and privately, before the group. |

## From the candidate

| They say | It usually means | Forcing question |
|---|---|---|
| "Really interesting, I'd love to learn more." | Open, not sold. Motive not yet found. | "What would have to be true about this role for you to leave where you are?" |
| "I need to think about it." | An objection they have not voiced. | "What's the one thing you're weighing up?" |
| "What's the package range again?" first thing after | Money is the lead motive, or the role did not land. | "Setting package aside, how did the role itself sit with you?" |
| "I've got a few other things going on." | Another process is ahead of yours. | "Where are you in those, and where do we sit?" |
| "They seemed great." | No connection formed with a specific person. | "Who on the panel would you most want to work for, and why?" |
| "It went well, I think." | Unsure how they performed. May be hiding a bad moment. | "Which question do you wish you'd answered differently?" |

## Sales-hire specifics

Sales candidates are trained to finish meetings on a positive note, so their debrief is always sunnier than the room was. Weight what they asked more than what they said. A candidate who asked about quota, territory, lead flow and ramp is evaluating the job. A candidate who asked only about culture and package is evaluating the offer.

Sales interviewers are trained to be won over by a strong close. When the whole panel is glowing about the role play and nobody can recall a specific number from the candidate's actual track record, the panel rated the pitch, not the pipeline.
