# Worked examples

Real cases from the operator's own pipeline, anonymized. None is a client win; there is no client win yet. They show the judgment calls that keep a solo pipeline honest.

## 1. Hiring signal read as insourcing: HOLD

**Input.** A direct-to-consumer home-goods brand posted two roles: an ERP ecosystem owner covering store, integration platform, warehouse and EDI monitoring and recovery, plus an inventory reconciliation analyst.

**Tempting read.** Integration pain, a budget for it, a perfect fit for an integration operator.

**What the screen found.**

- First screen: what gap is left after their tools and owner do their job? The brand already runs a native store-to-ERP connector and an integration platform. Any outside work would be one residual exception class (order or inventory state drifting between systems), not "fix our integrations."
- Intent: the hires are the tell. A company recruiting an owner for exactly this work is building the capability in-house. Hiring is evidence of insourcing, not a request for outside help.
- Evidence needed before qualifying: a 30 to 60 day sample of exceptions, redacted state records, a named owner willing to buy outside help. None existed.

**Decision.** Fit 50s across the board at best; explicit need UNKNOWN; funding UNKNOWN. HOLD, no outreach. Recheck only if the buyer ever asks for outside help on the residual exception class.

## 2. Platform adoption is not intent: no-fit

**Input.** A multi-location home-services company had adopted its field-service platform's enterprise hub and a centralized contact center.

**Tempting read.** Big platform change, lots of integration work, growth.

**What the screen found.** Adopting the vendor's own hub is a mature native-first strategy and shows in-house capability. Nothing in the evidence named a gap the platform left.

**Decision.** Kept unqualified with the reason preserved: acquisition or platform adoption alone is not intent. A sibling case, an insurance brokerage growing fast with no named system or problem, went to HOLD for the same reason.

## 3. Relevance mistaken for qualification: batch withdrawn

**What happened.** A drafting batch proposed three accounts for outreach because their businesses looked plausibly relevant to the operator's services.

**What went wrong.** All three already carried a HOLD disposition in the pipeline ledger, discovered only after the batch was assembled.

**Fix.** The batch was withdrawn before anything was sent. Annotations were corrected with the history preserved, not overwritten.

**Lesson.** Read dispositions and contact history before drafting, every time. "Looks relevant" is not a score.

## 4. The first live round: denominators matter

**What was sent.** In the first round across channels: between 5 and 10 marketplace proposals (the exact count was not logged), emails to 10 people, each followed up twice, and one direct message on a social platform.

**What came back.** The direct message drew a soft, friendly reply, then silence after the next message. No paid engagement came from the round.

**What changed.**

- Log the exact count of every send. "5 to 10" cannot feed the every-10-attempts review gate.
- One follow-up after five business days, not two.
- A soft reply is a reply, not a qualified conversation. Score the person with the rubric before investing more messages; without explicit need and a next step, it stays HOLD.
- A small round with no paid result is a channel and proof question, not evidence of no demand.

## Edge cases from marketplace postings

| Posting | Read it as | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting-integration fault, decent hourly budget, "minimum payment if it works" | Unfunded | Pass, or offer one funded diagnostic milestone and score the response |
| Broad multi-month build for a regulated buyer, large fixed budget | Complexity warning, not a prize | Pass unless it can be bounded to one deliverable within proven capability |
| Mid-size document workflow, solid budget, demands two production examples of the exact project | Trust barrier for a zero-review profile | Bid only if self-built proof genuinely matches; never invent the examples |
| Ongoing "IT person" at 30+ hrs/week | Staffing seat | Hard stop |
