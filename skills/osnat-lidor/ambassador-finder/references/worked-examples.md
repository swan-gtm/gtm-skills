# Worked examples

Three patterns that recur when screening ambassador candidates, and how the rubric
resolves each.

## Worked - the ex-operator from a market leader

**Signal:** former VP-level at a recognized leader in the field, now consulting. A
moderate following, not a huge one. Posts occasionally about the space.

**Screen:** field experience *confirmed*, independence *confirmed* (consulting), seniority
*confirmed*, reach *likely* (needs a manual check), publishes content *confirmed*.

**Why it works:** the market leader's reputation transfers. To the target audience this
person is "someone who did this at the company everyone benchmarks against" - that framing
carries further than a much larger follower count would. They are easy to recruit (there is
institutional credibility to trade on) and easy to deploy (the audience defers to them).

**Lesson:** ex-employees of a market leader are the highest-yield seam. Search these first.

## Flopped - the visible consultant with no scale behind them

**Signal:** 15k+ followers, posts constantly about the field, sounds credible.

**What the screen caught, and a follower-count ranking would have missed:** every company
in the work history was either very small or their own consultancy. No experience operating
at scale. To a buyer audience they read as a peer sharing opinions, not an authority - so
they were ineffective both to recruit (no institutional credibility to draw on) and to
deploy (the audience does not defer to them).

**Lesson:** check the size and type of the companies in the work history *before* the
follower count. This is the criterion that separates an ambassador from an influencer.

## Edge case - out of field, but the connection carries it

**Signal:** not in the company's specific field. But genuine expertise in an adjacent area,
a strong relevant network, a following that overlapped heavily with the target audience,
and a real prior connection to the space.

**Call:** included, despite failing criterion 1 as written. Adjacent credibility plus
audience overlap plus a warm connection cleared the bar that field experience alone would
normally set. Without the overlap and the connection, this candidate would not have been
approached.

**Lesson:** criterion 1 is a strong default, not an absolute. An out-of-field candidate has
to clear it another way - audience overlap with the ICP plus credibility the target
audience already recognizes. Absent both, hold the line.
