# Program screening rubric

Read this when screening programs in Step 3. Score every program on the same criteria, record the evidence for each, and keep credibility value and citation value apart. A program can be strong on one and weak on the other. Notoriety is estimated separately in `budget-and-notoriety-ranking.md` and never replaces this screen.

## Criteria

| Criterion | What to look for | Strong signal | Weak signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer relevance | Do the company's buyers know, read or trust the program? | Buyers, analysts or customers mention it; peers display it | Only entrants and vendors talk about it |
| Judging transparency | Published criteria, named judges, a described process | Criteria and judges are listed, and judging is independent of sponsorship | No criteria, anonymous judges, or "winners selected by the organizer" |
| Entry mechanics | Cost, effort, word limits, evidence demanded | Clear cost and a reasonable effort for the value | Fees that rise with sponsorship tiers, or surprise charges after winning |
| Eligibility fit | Company size, region, product category, stage, cycle dates | The company clearly qualifies in a named category | A stretch fit, or categories that do not match any use case |
| Winner publication | Are winners listed on a public, linkable page? | A permanent winners page with company names | Winners announced only to entrants or only in a paid supplement |
| Past winners | Who won in earlier cycles? | Peers and respected companies appear, and not every entrant wins | Nearly every entrant wins, or winners are mostly sponsors |
| Citation value | Do search results and AI answers cite this program or its winners page? | The program or its winners page appears in sources for the category's buyer questions | Never appears |
| Longevity | Has it run for several cycles? | Consistent cycles and a stable organizer | New, unproven, or the organizer changes name each cycle |

## Checking citation value

1. Write five to ten questions a buyer in each use case would ask, such as "best [category] for [situation]."
2. Ask them in search and in the AI assistants buyers use.
3. Note every source cited or listed, and whether any is an awards page, a best-of list or a recognition report.
4. Prefer programs, or the publications that run them, that appear.

Do not assume a program is cited because it is famous. Record the date of the check, since cited sources change.

## Red flags

- the organizer sells entry, tickets, trophies and advertising together, and winners are those who buy them;
- there are dozens of categories with very few entrants;
- the entry form asks for payment before eligibility is confirmed;
- the program promises coverage or a winners feature in exchange for a purchase;
- no public record of previous winners.

Flag these clearly. A pay-to-play program can still be worth entering for a specific buyer audience, but the shortlist must say so and let a human decide.

## Tiers

- **Enter:** strong on buyer relevance and eligibility, credible judging, and a public winners page.
- **Maybe:** fits well but is weak on one criterion, with the weakness stated.
- **Skip:** weak on relevance, transparency or fit, or shows red flags with no offsetting value.

Give one line of reasoning for every tier decision.

## Evidence labels

Mark each criterion **confirmed** (seen on the program's own pages or in independent sources), **likely** (strong circumstantial signal) or **unverified** (no evidence either way). Never present a criterion as met without evidence.
