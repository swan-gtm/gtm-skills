# Question bank

Options run from what good looks like down to the biggest gap. Show them in this order. Every question also accepts a typed answer: match it to the closest option, or ask again if it cannot be placed.

Each question names the dimension it informs, what good looks like, an example of good, and the 30-day move to use if it turns up as a gap. Use the example in the readout when that question drives a placement or a move, adapted to the leader's company.

## Opening (every role)

### J1. Where is your team on its AI GTM transformation journey?

Dimension: Systems. This is the self-placement; the placement checks in SKILL.md test it.

1. Shared AI layer. Several teams build and run agents on one foundation with permissions, monitoring and shared context.
2. AI as an action engine. Agents run defined workflows and skills when something triggers them.
3. AI as an answer engine. AI is connected to some systems and answers questions when someone asks.
4. Mostly manual. Research, list building and outreach are done by hand or in traditional tools.

- Sufficient when: a typed answer can be placed on one of the four. If not, ask which of the four is closest, naming them.
- Good looks like: several teams run agents on one shared foundation with permissions, monitoring and shared context.
- Example: marketing's event follow-up, sales' inbound qualification and CS's renewal prep all read the same account context, and each team changes its own workflow without a ticket.
- 30-day move: pick one repeatable workflow and run it as a triggered agent with an owner, an approval boundary and an outcome metric.

### P1. Think about the new pipeline you created in the last 6 months. Roughly what % came from each source?

Typed only. Prompt with the sources: inbound (website, forms, content, SEO), outbound (SDR or AE prospecting), LinkedIn and social, events and webinars, partners and referrals, product-led signups, existing customers. Example answer: 50% inbound, 25% outbound, 15% LinkedIn, 10% events.

- Sufficient when: it names sources with a share for each and covers most of the pipeline. "100% outbound" passes; "mostly inbound" or "a mix" does not.
- Use: the largest source is their dominant channel and decides which play leads the 30-day plan.

### P2. What are your top priorities for the next two quarters? Pick all that apply.

Multi-select. Options by role:

- Marketing: Increase pipeline / Increase revenue from marketing / Expand into a new channel (LinkedIn, events, partners) / Improve inbound conversion / Speed up lead follow-up with sales / Prove marketing's impact on revenue / Launch or scale ABM / Scale content with AI
- Sales: Increase pipeline / Increase revenue and hit quota / Expand outbound (email, LinkedIn, phone) / Raise win rates / Shorten sales cycles / Ramp new reps faster / Improve forecast accuracy / Get the team using AI day to day
- RevOps: Increase pipeline coverage / Trusted CRM data / Launch new channels or motions faster / Build signal routing and lead scoring / Improve forecast accuracy / Consolidate the tool stack / Roll out AI agents safely / Prove attribution
- Customer success: Increase net revenue retention / Reduce churn / Grow expansion revenue / Faster onboarding and time to value / Scale CS without adding headcount / Better health scoring / Smoother sales-to-CS handoffs / Customer advocacy and referrals
- Founder or CEO: Increase pipeline / Increase revenue / Expand into a new channel or market / Grow without adding headcount / Prove ROI on AI / Build a repeatable pipeline engine / Improve forecast predictability / Hit board or fundraise goals

- Sufficient when: at least one is picked, or a typed priority names a concrete goal ("grow" is too vague; "grow our LinkedIn motion" passes).
- Use: decides which motion gets the logic test and how the gaps are ranked.

### Q10. Who in your organization can build a GTM workflow today?

Dimension: People

1. Anyone in GTM, inside guardrails
2. RevOps only
3. Engineering
4. Nobody

- Sufficient when: a typed answer names who builds (a role, a team or a number of people). "It depends" is asked again.
- Good looks like: authorized operators in every function build inside clear permissions, tests and approvals.
- Example: a marketing ops manager builds the webinar follow-up workflow from an approved template on Monday; RevOps reviews what it can write to the CRM, and it runs on Wednesday's webinar.
- 30-day move: define builder roles and release one safe self-service path before widening access.

### Q19. What is the one number you are measured on this quarter?

Free text. Offer two or three options that fit the role and accept their own: marketing: pipeline sourced, MQL to SQL conversion; sales: new ARR, win rate; RevOps: forecast accuracy, pipeline coverage; customer success: net revenue retention, gross churn; founder: ARR growth, burn multiple.

- Good looks like: one business outcome with a baseline, a target, an owner and a period.
- Example: "Marketing-sourced pipeline, $4M this quarter against $3.1M last quarter, owned by the VP Marketing."
- Use: every move in the readout names this number and how it moves it.

## Marketing

### Q11. What is your current process for turning anonymous website visitors into meetings and pipeline?

Dimension: Systems

1. Identified, researched and worked automatically, with outreach sent
2. Identified, researched and routed with outreach drafted; the rep approves
3. Identified and dropped into Slack, a CRM task or a report
4. We don't identify anonymous visitors

- Good looks like: a qualified visitor is identified, researched for fit and context, and worked right away: automatically when confidence is high, with one-click rep approval otherwise.
- Example: a target account's VP visits the pricing page twice on Tuesday morning; by noon the owning AE has a brief on the account and the visitor, and a drafted note to approve.
- 30-day move: build one visitor-to-meeting workflow with an ICP filter, ownership rules, a response-time target and a clear line between automatic sends and rep approval.

- Example: a lead comes in at 3:10pm; the rep sends a researched first touch at 3:35pm, and the weekly report shows 96% of leads worked within the hour, by rep.
- 30-day move: set a speed-to-lead target, draft the first touch automatically, and report follow-up rate by rep weekly.

### M2. When a high-intent person or account does not convert, what happens next?

### M5. How consistently does AI-generated content follow your approved positioning and claims?

## Sales

## Production readiness (one per interview, adaptive)

### N1. How do you decide what company and customer data an AI GTM workflow may access?

Dimension: Systems

1. Policy by data class, with least privilege and audit logs
2. Approved tools and manual review
3. Team-by-team judgment
4. No defined rule

- Good looks like: access follows data class, least privilege, purpose and retention rules, with audit logs.
- Example: the renewal agent can read usage and support tickets but not billing details; security approved the data classes once, and the audit log shows every read.
- 30-day move: classify the data one workflow uses and remove access broader than its purpose.

### N2. When a model, prompt, vendor or integration changes, how do you know your GTM workflows still work?

Dimension: Process

1. Automated tests and alerts
2. Scheduled regression checks
3. Someone spot-checks outputs
4. We find out when users complain

- Good looks like: representative tests run on every relevant change, with thresholds, alerts and rollback.
- Example: a model update changes how first-touch emails open; twenty saved test cases catch it the same day, before a single prospect sees one.
- 30-day move: build a small regression set from real cases and run it whenever a dependency changes.

### N3. Who owns an AI GTM workflow after it launches?

Dimension: People

1. A named business owner, with technical support and service levels
2. RevOps or IT owns the queue
3. The original builder maintains it informally
4. No clear owner

- Good looks like: a named business owner owns the outcome; a technical owner owns reliability and changes.
- Example: the demand gen lead owns the inbound agent's meeting rate; RevOps owns its uptime; when the builder leaves, nothing stops.
- 30-day move: add an owner, a service level, a review cadence and retirement criteria to every production workflow.

### N4. Can you see the cost of an AI workflow per useful business outcome?

Dimension: Data

1. Cost per accepted outcome and downstream result
2. Cost per run or per user
3. Total vendor spend only
4. No

- Good looks like: cost is measured per accepted output and tied to meetings, pipeline, retention or expansion.
- Example: the event follow-up workflow costs a known amount per approved email and per meeting booked, and that number sits next to the cost of an SDR doing the same.
- 30-day move: instrument data, vendor and review cost for one workflow and link it to an outcome.

### N5. Before an AI output updates the CRM or reaches a customer, how is quality controlled?

Dimension: Process

1. Risk-based tests, confidence thresholds and approval gates
2. Rules plus human review
3. Occasional spot checks
4. No standard control

- Good looks like: controls match the risk of the action: validation, confidence thresholds, approvals, logging and rollback.
- Example: research notes write to the CRM automatically; emails to new prospects need rep approval; anything to an open opportunity or a customer stays human.
- 30-day move: classify actions by risk into automatic, reviewed and human-only.

### N6. Can you connect AI-assisted GTM work to pipeline, revenue, retention or expansion?

### N7. When an AI system recommends an account action, can the user see why?

### N8. How do you decide whether to buy, build or staff a GTM workflow?
