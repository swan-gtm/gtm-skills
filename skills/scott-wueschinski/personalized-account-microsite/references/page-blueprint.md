# Page blueprint

One page, six blocks, readable in under three minutes. Total copy of about 400 to 600 words.

## The six blocks

| # | Block | Length | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Their situation | 2 to 3 sentences | Opens with the account's own words or the strongest sourced fact. The headline names the account and the specific topic, never a generic greeting |
| 2 | What we noticed | 2 or 3 hypotheses, about 50 words each | Fact, inferred priority, cost of inaction. Each links its source |
| 3 | Our point of view | 1 short paragraph | The one change worth making, and why now |
| 4 | Proof | 1 or 2 items | The closest match to their situation: same problem, similar scale. One relevant example beats five logos |
| 5 | A first step | 3 to 5 lines | What the first 30 to 90 days would look like, scoped small enough to say yes to |
| 6 | One call to action | 1 line and a button | A specific, low-commitment ask: a 20-minute conversation about the strongest hypothesis |

## Copy rules

- Headline pattern: the account name plus the specific topic. "Northwind's regional expansion and the routing problem that comes with it", not "Welcome, Northwind".
- Second person throughout block 1 and block 2. The page is about them; the seller's company first appears in block 4.
- No capability lists. If a capability matters, it appears inside the point of view or the first step.
- Every number on the page is either theirs (sourced) or from the proof item (attributed).
- Plain language. If the reader's own leadership would not use a term, do not use it.

## Design constraints

- Match the reader's context: clean, fast and readable on a phone, because executives often open links between meetings.
- Use the seller's brand, with the account's name in text. Do not use the account's logo or trademarks without permission.
- No autoplay video, no pop-ups, no gated content. The page earns the meeting by giving value freely.
- Unlisted and not indexed by search engines. The page is for one reader, not for the public.
- Analytics only through the team's own systems, and no form that collects more than the meeting request needs.

## The swap test

Before handoff, replace the account's name with a direct competitor's name everywhere and read every block.

| Block | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Their situation | The quote or fact is plainly wrong for the competitor |
| What we noticed | At least two of the hypotheses no longer make sense |
| Point of view | It depends on a fact specific to this account |
| Proof | It is matched to this account's problem and scale |
| First step | It names this account's teams, systems or initiatives |

Any block that still reads correctly gets rewritten with a specific fact, or cut.

## The delivery message

```
Subject: [the strongest hypothesis, in five words or fewer]

[Name], [the single most specific observation, one sentence].
[What that usually means, one sentence].
We put together a short page on what we would do about it, built for [account] only: [link]
Worth 20 minutes to pressure-test it?
```

The message must work without the click: a reader who never opens the link should still have learned something specific.
