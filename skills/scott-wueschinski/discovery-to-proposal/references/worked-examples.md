# Worked examples

Three illustrative cases. They are composites written to show the judgment, not records of specific deals.

## 1. The proposal that should not have been written

A mid-market software company asked for "something in writing" after one good call. The notes held a clear problem in the buyer's words and an enthusiastic champion. They held no impact number, no economic buyer, no decision date and no success measures: 2 of 6.

The tempting move was to send a capabilities overview with standard pricing "to keep momentum". The skill returned a gap list instead: four questions, and a recommendation to ask for a 30-minute follow-up with the champion's director. That call surfaced a quantified cost of about 40 hours a month in manual work and a budget cycle closing in six weeks. The proposal written after it opened with the director's own description of the problem.

**The judgment:** a request for a proposal is often a polite exit. Treat it as a request for a second meeting.

## 2. The price that outran the value

A services firm had full discovery: 6 of 6. The buyer quantified rework costs at roughly $150,000 a year, and the low case put first-year value at $90,000. The seller's standard package was $45,000, half the low-case value, well above the one-fifth line of $18,000.

Stretching the assumptions would have produced a number the buyer's finance team would reject. The proposal shrank the recommended option to one business unit at $17,500 (under the line), kept the full package as the broader option, and added a second confirmed value source the buyer had mentioned: shorter onboarding for new hires. The buyer chose the recommended option and expanded to a second unit two quarters later.

**The judgment:** when price outruns value, change the scope, not the math.

## 3. The champion who could not forward it

A proposal for an operations leader was well built but opened with three pages about the seller's methodology. The champion asked for "a shorter version for my CFO". Rewritten, the one-page summary led with the CFO-relevant number (working capital tied up in late reconciliations), the recommended option and price, and the decision date. The champion forwarded it unchanged, and it was the only page the CFO read before approving.

**The judgment:** write the summary for the person who was not in the room. If the champion has to rewrite it, it was written for the wrong reader.
