# Proposal structure and pricing

## Sections, in order

| # | Section | Length | What it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-page summary | 1 page | Problem, value, recommended option, price, decision date. Stands alone |
| 2 | What we heard | Half a page | The problem and its impact in the buyer's words, quoted where possible |
| 3 | What success looks like | Half a page | The buyer's success measures with a baseline and a target for each |
| 4 | The value | 1 page | The impact arithmetic, every input sourced, every assumption labeled |
| 5 | Options | 1 to 2 pages | Three options with scope, timeline, price and what each does not include |
| 6 | How we will work | 1 page | Phases, milestones, team on both sides, governance cadence |
| 7 | Mutual plan to signature | Half a page | Every step to kickoff with an owner on each side and a date |
| 8 | Risks and owners | Half a page | Each risk, its likelihood, its mitigation and its owner |
| 9 | Why us | Half a page | Two or three proof points relevant to this problem, at most |
| 10 | Terms | As needed | Payment, term, assumptions, change process |

"Why us" goes near the end on purpose. By the time a buyer reaches it, they should already believe the plan; proof confirms the belief and does not create it.

## Writing the value section

Show the arithmetic so a skeptical finance reviewer can check it in under a minute.

```
Problem: manual reconciliation of partner invoices
Volume: 1,200 invoices a month (buyer, second call)
Time per invoice: 18 minutes (buyer estimate, confirmed by their ops lead)
Loaded cost per hour: $65 (assumption; buyer to confirm with finance)
Annual cost: 1,200 x 12 x 0.3 hours x $65 = $280,800
Target reduction: 60% (buyer's stated goal)
First-year value: $168,480
```

When an input is uncertain, show the range rather than a point: a low case from the most conservative input and a high case from the buyer's stated goal. Price against the low case.

## Pricing the three options

| Option | Scope | Price logic |
|---|---|---|
| Focused | Solves the confirmed problem and nothing else | Priced from its scope; often the option a cautious buyer starts with |
| Recommended | Focused plus the next most valuable outcome from discovery | At or below one fifth of first-year value from the low case |
| Broader | The full path, phased | Shows where the relationship goes; rarely bought first, often bought second |

Rules:

- Price each option from its scope. Do not build options by discounting one another.
- State what each option excludes. Exclusions prevent scope disputes later and make the options easier to compare.
- Mark one option as recommended, and give the reason in one sentence tied to a success measure.
- If the recommended option's price exceeds one fifth of low-case first-year value, either find a second value source the buyer has confirmed, or shrink the first phase. Do not stretch the assumptions.

## The mutual plan

```
Step                          Buyer owner        Seller owner     Date
Proposal review call          Champion           Account lead     Week 1
Economic buyer review         VP Operations      Account lead     Week 2
Security questionnaire        IT security lead   Solutions lead   Weeks 2 to 3
Procurement and legal         Procurement        Deal desk        Weeks 3 to 5
Signature                     VP Operations      Account lead     Week 5
Kickoff                       Ops lead           Delivery lead    Week 6
```

Work backward from the buyer's decision date. If the steps do not fit before it, say so in the review call; a plan that cannot hit its date is a forecast risk.
