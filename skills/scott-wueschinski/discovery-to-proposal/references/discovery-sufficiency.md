# Discovery sufficiency

Score each element met or not met from the call notes and transcripts. Met means the buyer said it, and you can point to where. A seller's inference scores as not met.

## The six elements

| Element | Met when the notes contain | Not met looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed problem | The buyer describing the problem in their own words, and agreeing to a summary of it played back | "They seem to struggle with reporting" |
| Quantified impact | A number the buyer gave or confirmed: hours, cost, revenue at risk, error rate, cycle time | "Big impact on the team" |
| Economic buyer | The name of the person who approves the spend, and evidence they have engaged (attended, or the champion relayed their words) | "Our VP will want to see it" with no name |
| Decision process and date | The steps to approval (who reviews, legal, procurement, security) and a date the buyer wants the outcome by | "We'll decide soon" |
| Success measures | What the buyer will measure in the first 90 days to call this a success | "Make things better" |
| Cost of doing nothing | What happens if they change nothing this year, in the buyer's words | No discussion of the status quo |

## The verdict

| Elements met | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 6 of 6 | Write the full proposal |
| 5 of 6 | Write it, and close the missing element in the review meeting; mark it in the draft |
| 4 or fewer | Do not write the proposal. Return the gap list |

Two elements override the count. **Quantified impact** missing means there is nothing to price against; return the gap list even at 5 of 6. **Economic buyer** missing means the proposal will be read by someone who was never in the room; write it only if the champion commits to a meeting with the economic buyer before it is sent.

## Questions that close each gap

Ask in the next conversation, not in the proposal.

- **Problem:** "If I played back what I heard as the core problem, would you say I have it right? What did I miss?"
- **Impact:** "How often does this happen in a month, and what does each occurrence cost you in hours or dollars? Ballpark is fine."
- **Economic buyer:** "Who, besides you, has to say yes to a spend like this? What will they want to see?"
- **Decision process:** "Walk me through the last time you bought something of this size. What steps did it go through, and how long did each take?"
- **Success measures:** "Ninety days after we start, what would you need to see to tell your boss this was the right call?"
- **Cost of doing nothing:** "If nothing changes this year, what happens? Who feels it first?"

## The gap list format

Return one line per missing element: the element, what the notes contain today, and the question that closes it. Add a recommendation on whether to ask for a short follow-up call or to cover it in a written reply. A gap list is a useful deliverable; sending it to the account owner is how weak discovery gets fixed before it costs a cycle.
