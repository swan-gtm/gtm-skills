# Live Demo Prep Reference

Mode-specific guidance for producing live demo prep materials.

## Talk Track Format

The talk track is NOT a verbatim script. It's phase-by-phase bullet-level guidance that a seller can internalize and deliver naturally. Each phase gets:

- **Intent**: One sentence on what this phase accomplishes.
- **Key points**: 2-4 bullets of what to say, phrased as the core idea (not exact words). Example: "Connect poor forecasting to cash trapped in inventory -- either overstocked and expiring, or understocked and losing orders. Both leak margin." The seller decides how to say it.
- **Screen**: Which screen to be on and what to show. Specific enough to follow without rehearsal.
- **Checkpoint**: Where to pause and engage (see Discovery Checkpoints below).
- **If/then branches**: "If they ask about [X], go to [screen]. If they push back on [Y], use [objection handle #N]."

Do not write the talk track as prose paragraphs. Sellers scan, they don't read. Bullets, short phrases, and clear labels.

## Discovery Checkpoint Placement

Place 3-5 discovery checkpoints across the demo. A checkpoint is a planned pause where the seller stops showing and starts listening. Winning demos (per Gong research) have frequent speaker switches -- roughly every minute or two.

**Where to place them:**

1. **After Phase 1 (Outcome)**: "Is this the kind of result you're looking for? What does [outcome] look like in your world today?" -- Validates you're solving the right problem before investing 15 minutes in use cases.

2. **After Phase 2 (Magic)**: "Does that match how you're doing it today? What's your current process for [the thing the magic replaces]?" -- Surfaces the real incumbent process, which may differ from assumptions.

3. **After each use case in Phase 3**: "Is this the kind of thing you're dealing with?" / "How are you handling this today?" / "Who on your team would be using this part?" -- Checks resonance and identifies stakeholder fit.

4. **Before Phase 4 (Depth)**: "What questions do you have about what you've seen so far?" -- Earns the right to go deep. If no questions, skip Phase 4 and close.

5. **Objection-triggered**: Any time the prospect raises a doubt or concern -- pause, listen fully, respond, then confirm before moving on.

**Checkpoint discipline:** Do not ask a checkpoint question and then immediately keep talking. Pause. Wait for the answer. If the answer reveals an unexpected pain, acknowledge it and adjust the remaining demo flow on the fly.

## Objection Handle Structure

Prepare 3-5 likely objections based on audience role, industry, buying stage, and alternatives from Phase 0. Each handle follows a three-part structure:

1. **Acknowledge**: Mirror the concern back without defensiveness. "So you're asking whether [restate their concern]..."
2. **Reframe**: Connect the objection to the demo's opinion or a broader business reality. "The reason that matters is [tie to opinion]. Here's what we see with companies like yours..."
3. **Evidence**: Show proof -- a screen, a metric, a customer example. Not a claim. "Let me show you how [similar company] handled exactly this."

Prepare handles as thinking frameworks, not scripted rebuttals. The acknowledge-reframe-evidence structure keeps the conversation collaborative.

**Common objection categories:**

- "We already have something that does this" (incumbent/status quo defense)
- "How is this different from [competitor]?" (competitive differentiation)
- "This looks great but we don't have the resources to implement" (adoption/change management)
- "What about [edge case / unusual workflow]?" (depth request -- route to Phase 4)
- "Our data is [messy / siloed / sensitive]" (integration/security concern)

## Fallback Planning

Things break. The fallback plan ensures the demo's opinion still gets delivered even when the product can't be shown live.

**Three layers:**

1. **Screenshot deck**: 5-8 annotated screenshots walking the Outcome-Magic-Use Cases-Close arc. Not a marketing deck -- actual product screenshots with callouts. Have it open in a background tab before the call.

2. **Narrative pivot**: A version delivered purely as story, no screens. "Let me walk you through what a [persona] at a company like yours experiences in a typical week..." Leans on the demo's opinion and before/after structure.

3. **Partial recovery**: If only part of the product is down, know which use cases still work. Don't demo a broken feature and narrate around it -- skip, note, and offer a follow-up.

**Before every live demo:**

- Open the demo environment 10 minutes early and verify data and key screens work.
- Have the screenshot deck in a background tab.
- Know which use case to cut first if time runs short (cut the least important use case, never the outcome or close).
- Test screen sharing before the prospect joins.

## Attendee Role Mapping

When multiple stakeholders attend, map each use case and checkpoint to the most relevant person. Address by name: "Maria, this is the part I think matters most for your team -- the production scheduling view."

If you know the attendee list from Phase 0:
- Assign each use case a primary audience member.
- Plan at least one checkpoint directed at each attendee by name.
- Executive buyer: Phase 1 (Outcome) and Phase 5 (Close) speak to them. Use cases speak to the champion/user.
- IT/security: Phase 4 (Depth) is their section. Don't front-load technical details for the whole room.
