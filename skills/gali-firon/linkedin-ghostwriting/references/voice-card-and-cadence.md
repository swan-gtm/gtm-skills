# Voice card format and starter questions

The voice card is the single most load-bearing input to this skill. It is what stops a ghostwritten post from sounding like a brand instead of the person. Build one per exec before drafting, from their actual posting history and how they actually talk - never from a guess about how "a CEO" sounds.

## What a voice card holds

Keep it to one page. Every field earns its place by changing what gets drafted.

| Field | What goes in it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Diction and register | How formal or blunt they are, sentence length, words they reach for, words they never use, whether they swear, whether they use "I" or "we" | This is what makes a draft sound like them at the sentence level |
| Arguments they own | The two or three positions they return to and are known for | Keeps posts on the beat their audience follows them for |
| Off-limits | Topics, claims, or framings they will not touch, plus anything legal or IR has flagged | A hard gate; feeds the anti-repeat off-limits map |
| Proof they can stand on | The data, wins, and first-hand experience they can speak to without hedging | Grounds posts in something only they can say |
| Format habits | Do they post long or short, use lists or prose, ask questions, share links | Matches the draft to how their feed already reads |
| Gold-standard posts | One or two of their best past posts, pasted in full | The concrete target a draft is measured against |
| Hard nos | Tone or device they hate (contrarian openers, humblebrags, emoji, hashtags) | Prevents the drafts that get rejected on sight |

## Starter questions to build one

When the history is thin or the exec is new to posting, interview them (or whoever owns their voice). Ask, in their words:

1. Whose posts do you read and think "I wish I'd written that"? What makes them good?
2. What are the two or three things you want to be known for saying?
3. What would you never post about, even if it would do numbers?
4. What can you claim from first-hand experience that most people in your seat cannot?
5. Show me a post of yours you were proud of. What made it yours?
6. What makes you cringe when you see it on LinkedIn?
7. Long and considered, or short and sharp? Question at the end, or a statement?
8. "I" or "we"? How formal? Would you ever swear, use an emoji, use a hashtag?

Pull answers 1, 5, and 6 straight into the gold-standard and hard-nos fields; they carry the most signal about voice.

## Keeping the card current

The card is not static. Each time the exec rewrites a draft before posting, that rewrite is a better sample of their real voice than anything already on the card - fold it in and, if it is strong, make it the new gold-standard post. Over a few cycles the card converges on how they actually write, and the drafts need less editing.

## Cadence

Posting rhythm and the anti-repeat cooldown windows live in the companion `anti-repeat-and-cadence.md` reference. The short version: use the ledger's last-live date per exec as the authoritative cadence clock, and hold to a steady rhythm rather than posting in bursts.
