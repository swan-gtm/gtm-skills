# Rubrics

Four rubrics, each on an anchored 1–4 scale. Four points, not five, so there is no safe middle: every score says "reliable enough" (3–4) or "not" (1–2). The timeline, forces, progress dimensions and pathways scored here are defined in `switch-model.md`.

## How to tailor a rubric

The templates below are generic on purpose. Before handing one to a user:

1. **Name the decision.** Put the research brief's decision sentence at the top. Criteria that don't serve it get cut.
2. **Replace generic anchors with market specifics.** "Considered a real alternative" becomes "Named a specific competitor, a spreadsheet or 'nothing'." "Reached value" becomes the product's actual activation event.
3. **Weight criteria.** Mark the two or three that matter most for this study and say why. Default weights are equal.
4. **Set thresholds.** Say what score qualifies, prioritizes, or disqualifies — and what happens to evidence below threshold.
5. **Add one worked example.** Score one real participant, guide or transcript and show the reasoning. Examples are what get copied.
6. **Keep it on one page.** If a rubric needs a manual, nobody will use it in the room.

Deliver a tailored rubric as a table the user can paste into a sheet: criterion, weight, anchors for 1–4, notes.

## A. Participant fit

**Use:** screen survey or CRM records and prioritize who to invite. Score from screener answers and account data, before scheduling.

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision proximity** | Not involved in the decision | Aware of it, not involved | Part of the evaluation | Led or owned the decision |
| **Recency** | >12 months ago | 6–12 months | 3–6 months | <3 months (win-loss: <2 weeks; churn: as soon after cancelling as possible) |
| **Segment match** | Outside every target segment | Edge of a segment | Clearly in a segment, over-quota | Clearly in a segment that still needs interviews |
| **Best-customer signal** *(best-customer studies)* | Discounting, low use, heavy support | Paying, light use | Paying, regular use, little hand-holding | Paying without friction, regular use, retained or expanded |
| **Switch story** | No prior approach or alternative named | Vague prior approach | Named a prior tool or process | Named a prior approach *and* other options considered |
| **Independence** | Personal relationship with interviewer or reseller | Frequent contact with team | Normal customer relationship | No prior relationship with the interviewer |

**Thresholds:** decision proximity and recency must be ≥3. Invite in descending total score within each segment until the quota is filled.

## B. Guide quality

**Use:** review a draft guide before the first interview — your own or someone else's.

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tied to a decision** | No decision or research questions stated | Research questions vague or numerous | Decision and 3–5 research questions stated | Plus a map showing which probes answer each research question |
| **Story-based** | A list of direct questions | Mostly direct questions, one story prompt | Opens with a story prompt; probes mostly follow the story | Story prompt opens at the first thought and is scoped to the decision; probes organized by switch-timeline phase |
| **Past, not future** | Hypotheticals and "would you" throughout | Several hypotheticals | One or two, flagged as optional | None; every question anchors to a past instance |
| **Neutral wording** | Leading questions or product pitch in guide | Some leading phrasing | Neutral; product mentioned only late | Neutral; product not mentioned until the participant brings it up |
| **Covers resistance** | No questions on worries, alternatives or habit | Asks about alternatives only, or asks for forces in the abstract | Probes for anxiety and alternatives inside the deciding phase | Force probes for all four forces, used once a force surfaces; alternatives include building it themselves and doing nothing |
| **Comparable** | No must-cover topics | Must-cover topics unclear | Must-cover topics listed | Must-cover topics listed with drill-downs for recurring themes |
| **Right length** | >60 min or >25 scripted questions | 45–60 min | 30–45 min, ≤15 core probes | Fits the time with room to follow energy; ratings moved to survey |
| **Language harvest** | No questions on how they'd describe it | One | Asks how they'd describe it to a peer | Plus what they'd search for and their word for the problem |

**Threshold:** no criterion below 2; "story-based" and "past, not future" must be ≥3 before fielding.

## C. Interview quality

**Use:** after each interview, score from the recording or transcript. Two jobs: decide how much weight this interview's evidence carries, and coach the interviewer. Score it before you code it.

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story specificity** | All generalities ("we usually…") | A few specifics, mostly general | A specific instance; first thought or decision pinned to a moment | First thought, the event that started active looking, and the decision all pinned to moments, with people, alternatives and artifacts |
| **Interviewer neutrality** | Pitched the product or argued | Several leading questions | One or two mild leads | No leading; product only discussed when raised |
| **Talk ratio** | Interviewer talked ≥50% | 30–50% | 15–30% | <15% |
| **Follow-up depth** | Moved on after first answers | Occasional follow-up | Consistent "what happened next?" probing | Followed energy to root causes; dug under every feature request |
| **Fluff handling** | Accepted compliments and hypotheticals as data | Sometimes redirected | Usually anchored to past instances | Consistently deflected compliments, anchored fluff, dug under ideas |
| **Forces captured** | No anxiety or habit evidence, or forces only in answer to abstract questions | Anxiety or habit mentioned, not explored | Anxiety or habit explored inside a specific event | All four forces tied to specific events, including who resisted and what was given up |
| **Coverage** | Missed most must-cover topics | Missed several | Covered must-cover topics where relevant | Covered them naturally inside the story |
| **Language captured** | No verbatims on problem or category | A few | Clear verbatims for problem and outcome | Plus how they'd describe it to a peer and what they'd search |

**Evidence weight:**
- **Strong** — story specificity and neutrality both ≥3
- **Usable** — both ≥2, total ≥18 of 32
- **Weak** — specificity or neutrality = 1. Keep the transcript, label findings from it as weak, and never let it carry a finding alone.

**Coaching note:** end each scored interview with one thing to keep doing and one thing to change, quoting the moment in the transcript.

## D. Evidence coding

**Use:** code what a transcript actually says. This is not a quality score — it's a structured extraction that makes cross-interview patterns visible. One row per interview in a shared sheet.

**Fields**

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Interview ID, date, interviewer | — |
| Type, segment | best-customer / win-loss / churn / non-switcher; segment |
| Evidence weight | From Rubric C |
| **Timeline** | Phases reached, with a date or season for each that was pinned |
| **First thought** | What set it off, in their words |
| **Event to active looking** | What turned noticing into searching |
| **What made it "today"** | The deadline or forcing event at deciding |
| **Prior approach** | What they used before: another vendor, a general-purpose tool, something built, a person, nothing |
| **Alternatives considered** | Every option named, including building it and doing nothing |
| **Push** | What was broken, with verbatim |
| **Pull** | What attracted them, with verbatim |
| **Anxiety** | What worried them about switching, with verbatim |
| **Habit** | What kept them on the old way, with verbatim |
| **Forces at deciding** | What tipped it, ranked by emphasis (win-loss: the decision drivers) |
| **Pathway** | The dominant push plus the dominant pull, in a short phrase |
| **Progress sought** | Functional / emotional / social, each in their words |
| **Progress realized** | What they can do now (best-customer) or failed to get (churn, loss) |
| **Language** | Problem nouns, workaround nouns, outcome verbs, search phrases, pain adjectives |
| **Channel** | How they first heard of us and of the alternatives |
| **Surprises** | Anything that contradicts current assumptions |

**Force strength** (score each of push, pull, anxiety, habit):

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not present |
| 1 | Mentioned in passing |
| 2 | Described with a specific instance |
| 3 | Described with a specific instance and clear consequences (cost, embarrassment, lost time, a deadline) |

Force strength makes pathways and segments comparable: if one group of churners averages 3 on push from never getting the basics working and another averages 3 on pull from a competitor's feature set, those are two different problems with two different fixes.

**Pathway coding:** name the pathway in the participant's terms ("outgrew the spreadsheet" + "wanted it working on day one"), not yours. Settle a shared list of pathway names after the first five or six interviews, then recode the early ones against it.

**Coding rules**
- Quote verbatim. Never paraphrase into the verbatim column.
- Code only what was said. Inferences go in a separate "analyst note" column, labeled.
- Two coders on the first three transcripts; compare and settle disagreements before coding the rest alone.

## Using scores together

```
A (fit)  → who gets interviewed
B (guide) → whether the guide is ready
C (quality) → how much each interview counts
D (coding) → what the interviews say, comparably
```

A finding in the report needs: at least three interviews in the pathway or segment coding the same force or theme, at least two of them **strong** on Rubric C, and a verbatim from each.
