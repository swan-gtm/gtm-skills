Summary: What AI Is Revealing About Women, Leadership, and Expertise

AI did not suddenly make accomplished women less capable. But it has introduced an entirely new vocabulary for skills many women leaders already use every day.

Giving clear direction became prompt engineering. Building repeatable processes became workflows. Providing context, managing ambiguity, asking better questions, and exercising judgment became the foundations of effective AI leadership.

So why do so many accomplished women still feel behind?

In this fireside conversation, Leah Russo, Vice President of Marketing at Clearco, and Allison Moore, CEO of Chief, will examine what the AI transition is revealing about confidence, credibility, experimentation, and who feels entitled to claim expertise.

They’ll explore the difference between learning genuinely new technology and recognizing the value of capabilities women have spent years developing. They’ll also discuss how leaders can create the psychological safety people need to experiment before they feel confident, and how to ensure AI reduces the burden of leadership instead of becoming one more thing women are expected to master.

This is not a conversation about becoming more technical or keeping up with every new tool. It is about learning the technology without allowing unfamiliar terminology to erase the evidence of your existing competence.

Attendees will leave with a clearer understanding of:

• Why feeling behind is not always the same as being behind
• Which leadership skills become more valuable as AI handles more execution
• How to build credibility when everyone is still learning
• Why experimentation must come before confidence
• How to use AI to create leverage and reduce mental load

Most importantly, they’ll leave with a more useful question than “Do I know enough?”

What am I ready to try next?
