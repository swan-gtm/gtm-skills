# Viral LinkedIn Lead Magnet Post

Fires when someone wants a LinkedIn post promoting a free resource, playbook,
template pack, or checklist through a "comment WORD to get it" mechanic, or
wants an underperforming one diagnosed and rewritten.

Produces: the post ready to paste, one media-direction line, a build-scope
note, and a first-comment draft when a shareable sample exists.

The formula is calibrated against a 100-post performance dataset (top 15 vs
bottom 50 by engagement). Read `references/voice-and-structure.md` before
writing any copy — it holds the structural rules and the calibration numbers
behind them.

## Step 1 — Intake

Collect these, extracting silently from what's already given and asking only
for the gaps:

1. The magnet — what it is, working name
2. What's inside — the actual deliverables
3. ICP — who it's for, in their words
4. The pain it solves, right now
5. Proof — real numbers or credentials only; never invent these. No proof →
   use an experience-based or contrarian hook instead
6. Comment word — their preference, or propose one, thematic to the magnet

## Step 2 — Qualify the magnet

Check the three rules in `references/titles-and-deliverables.md` (the $100
test, sub-hour consumption, ICP-specific). Fail one → say so plainly and
propose the fix before writing, usually by splintering one immediately-usable
piece out of a bigger asset. A viral post on a weak magnet burns trust; don't
skip this. Rename a vague magnet using the title formulas in the same file.

## Step 3 — Hook angles

Generate three hook blocks — hook line, free-claim parenthetical, pivot line —
using three different archetypes from `references/hook-angles.md`, matched to
the actual proof profile: an angle demanding a metric that doesn't exist is
disqualified. Present all three and let the person choose. On "just give me
the best one," pick it, state which and why in one line, and go straight to
Step 4. For multi-variant runs (up to five), cross each hook archetype with a
different narrative frame so variants read as genuinely different posts, not
one post re-hooked.

## Step 4 — Build the full post

Expand the chosen angle into the five-part structure and every voice rule in
`references/voice-and-structure.md`: outcome-first hook, free-claim
parenthetical, a pain block that pivots from "I/we" to "you", the deliverable
stack (rules in `references/titles-and-deliverables.md`), then CTA + P.S.

## Step 5 — Self-score, then deliver

Score the draft against the checklist in `references/voice-and-structure.md`
before showing it; fix any failure silently. Deliver the post, one
media-direction line (the visual must prove the hook's claim, never decorate
it), and a build-scope note that every deliverable bullet is now a promise the
resource must contain. If a shareable sample exists, draft the first comment
hosting it too — confirm publication rights on real client material, and check
any visible date still reads current.

## Rewrites and diagnosis

Score the existing post first, name the two or three highest-impact failures
in plain language, then rewrite — keep what already works; surgical fixes beat
full rewrites when the bones are good. On every returned edit, re-score
silently: the comment CTA, the P.S., and the free-claim parenthetical are the
usual casualties, and re-audit the deliverable stack against what the
fulfilment asset can actually send, since edits routinely add promises it
can't keep. Past ~1,600 characters, propose cuts that pay for the addition
rather than accepting the bloat.

## What good looks like

The single biggest finding from the calibration set: top and flopped posts
usually promote equally good resources — packaging is the variable, not the
offer. A good post opens on a concrete result, number, or named tool in line
one, never a topic or a question. It carries the free-claim parenthetical
within the first three lines. It names the reader's exact pain in their own
words and pivots to "you" before the deliverable list. The deliverable stack
is 4–6 bullets, each concrete enough that the ICP would comment on a public
post to get that specific thing. It closes on one comment word and a P.S.
that earns its place — a proof pointer, a trust line, or a connection note,
never filler. The common failure isn't a weak resource; it's explaining the
offer in prose instead of listing it, a vague magnet name, or a fabricated
number. Any invented metric, client result, or testimonial disqualifies the
post outright. It's ready when every number in it is real and the deliverable
stack matches exactly what the fulfilment file will send.

## Hard rules

- Never fabricate metrics, client results, or social proof — real numbers or
  none
- One post, one magnet, one CTA — never stack offers
- If a proof point sounds invented, ask before using it
- Match the person's own spelling convention (UK/US)
