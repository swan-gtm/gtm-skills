# Hook Angle Library

Seven archetypes, ranked roughly by ceiling in the calibration dataset.
Every post opens with exactly one. When generating the three-angle set in
Step 3, pick the three that best fit the user's *proof profile* — an angle
that demands a metric the user doesn't have is disqualified.

Exemplars below are original demonstrations of the pattern, not real posts.

## 1. Tool-powered result
A named AI tool + an outsized outcome. Highest ceiling when the tool is
having a moment. Requires: a real workflow and a genuine result.
> "Claude + one cron job now writes my entire content pipeline.
> (steal the full setup, completely free)"

## 2. Metric shock
One hard, precise number doing all the work. Requires: a real metric.
> "We booked 27 sales calls in 48 hours for one client...
> All from 1 LinkedIn post (grab the playbook free below)"

## 3. Before/after contrast
Two timestamped states, the gap is the hook. Requires: an honest before.
> "7 months of daily posting: 800 followers, 1-2 calls a month.
> 30 days of THIS system: 21 booked calls."

## 4. Curation flex
You did the tedious collecting so they don't have to. Requires: the
collection genuinely exists. Numbers make it ("166 posts across 8 niches").
> "I saved every viral giveaway post I found for 6 months.
> 166 of them. One database. It's yours..."

## 5. Contrarian pattern-interrupt
Open by attacking the default behaviour of the ICP. Works with zero
metrics — the strongest angle for users without proof points yet.
> "STOP automating individual tasks in 2026.
> It's why your 'AI stack' saved you 20 minutes and changed nothing."

## 6. Experiment reveal
You ran a controlled test; the result is counterintuitive. Requires: the
test actually happened.
> "I posted the exact same lead magnet twice.
> 1st time: 40 comments. 2nd time: 1,158.
> The only thing I changed..."

## 7. Borrowed authority
Apply a famous operator's framework to your channel and report what
happened. Requires: you genuinely applied it. The famous name carries
reach; your result carries trust.
> "In Feb I applied Hormozi's lead magnet framework to LinkedIn.
> In 3 weeks, here's what changed:"

## Selecting the three for Step 3

- User has strong metrics → lead with 1 or 2, add 3, add 5 for contrast.
- User has no metrics yet → 5, 4 (if they own a collection), 7.
- Magnet IS a collection/swipe file → 4 is near-mandatory as one of the
  three.
- A named AI tool is central to the magnet → 1 is near-mandatory.

Never present two angles from the same archetype.

## Narrative frames (for multi-variant runs)

Hook archetypes decide the *opening claim*; narrative frames decide *who
is telling the story and how*. Crossing the two is what keeps five variants
genuinely different rather than one post re-hooked five times.

- **The Mirror** — second person; the reader lives the scenario line by
  line and lands on "Sound familiar?" Strongest when the pain is a shared,
  repeated moment the ICP recognises instantly.
- **The Confession** — first person; admit the embarrassing cost you paid
  before the fix (the hours, the unbilled work, the night-before panic).
  Vulnerability reads as proof.
- **The Vivid Contrast** — one sharp metaphor carries the whole gap
  ("senior-partner thinking, ransom-note handover"). Pick one image and
  commit; two metaphors cancel each other.
- **The Intervention** — contrarian frame aimed at the reader's default
  behaviour. Pairs naturally with archetype 5; the frame supplies the
  "here's the loop I see everywhere" body.
- **The Fork** — two artefacts or outcomes side by side, present tense,
  no invented event. The safest frame when the user has no story or
  metrics — the contrast *is* the story.

In a multi-variant set, no two variants share a frame, and each gets its
name and a one-line mechanism when presented.
