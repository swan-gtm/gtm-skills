# Beyond the interview: learning speed and mindset

Interviews show what a candidate says they did. This page covers two tests that show who will actually do the work: the live feedback-and-repeat exercise, and the two mindset questions.

## Live exercise: the feedback-and-repeat test

Behavioral questions show what a candidate says they did. A live exercise shows how they think and behave now. The feedback-and-repeat test shows something more valuable: how fast they learn.

Use it for any role where judgment, communication, selling, coaching, leadership, or problem-solving matter. Tie the exercise to one or two critical outcomes on the scorecard.

### The protocol

1. **Set it up.** A realistic scenario from the role: a discovery call, a pipeline review with a struggling rep, a pricing objection, a board update. Same scenario and same brief for every candidate. Tell candidates in advance that there will be feedback and a second attempt.
2. **Attempt one.** Let them finish without interruption. Take notes on specific moments.
3. **Feedback.** Two or three points, specific and actionable. "You moved to the demo before confirming the problem" beats "slow down." Use the same feedback structure for every candidate.
4. **Questions.** Invite them. Then stay quiet.
5. **Attempt two.** Immediately, same scenario.
6. **Debrief.** Ask: "What did you change, and what would you still work on?"
7. **Score the delta.**

### What strong candidates do

They often ask before retrying:

- "Can you give me an example of what you mean?"
- "Which part would you prioritize?"
- "Where exactly did you notice that?"
- "Do you want me to change the structure, or just how I ask the question?"

Then they show the feedback. Not some of it. They visibly demonstrate that they heard it, understood it, and can turn instruction into different behavior.

The test reads four things at once: **listening, understanding, application, coachability.**

### Scoring

Score each dimension 1 to 4. Attempt one sets the baseline; the other five dimensions measure learning.

| Dimension | 4 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline capability | Attempt one meets the bar for the role | Attempt one has basic gaps |
| Listening | Restates the feedback accurately, unprompted | Restates part of it, or restates their own interpretation |
| Clarification | Asks precise questions that sharpen the feedback | Asks nothing, or asks for reassurance |
| Application | Every feedback point is visible in attempt two | One point applied, or none |
| Adaptability | Changes without losing what worked in attempt one | Changes one thing and breaks another |
| Self-awareness | Names what improved and what still needs work | Says it went well, or cannot say what changed |

### Reading the result

| First attempt | Second attempt | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Stronger | Capable and coachable. Rare; move fast |
| Weak | Clearly stronger | Steep learning curve. Often the highest-potential hire, especially into a strong coaching environment |
| Strong | Unchanged | Plateaued. Will perform at today's level and resist coaching |
| Weak | Unchanged | Do not hire. The issue is probably listening or willingness to change, not knowledge |

A weak second attempt is more revealing than a weak first one. A candidate who receives clear feedback and repeats the original behavior has shown you the gap is not knowledge.

**Do not hire the snapshot. Hire the trajectory.** The highest-potential candidate is not always the best first attempt. It may be the steepest improvement curve. Pair the result with the scorecard: competencies marked critical must exist on day one; supporting competencies can be learned, and the delta shows how fast.

## Mindset questions

Outcomes and competencies show whether a candidate can do the job. Two questions show who will be doing it. Ask them in every Type 1 loop and in every Type 2 screen, in this order, and never back to back with a technical question.

### Question 1: the horizon

**"Project forward 18 to 24 months. What is going to be different in your life?"**

Ask it about their life, not their career. Then stay silent. The first answer is often rehearsed; the second sentence is usually the real one.

**Listen for:**

| Signal | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon | Goals that reach beyond the role | Only success in this job |
| Scope | Personal and professional goals, connected | Title, compensation, or company outcomes only |
| Motivation | An intrinsic reason: something they want to build, learn, become, or provide | External markers: status, money, the next title |
| Disclosure | Volunteers something real about what drives them | Stays generic and safe |

**Why it matters.** A willingness to share a genuine motivator is a trust signal. People who can name what drives them tend to be candid about other hard things, including bad news. Candidates who answer only in role terms are not disqualified, but the absence of any intrinsic motivator is worth probing in references.

### Question 2: the qualifier

**"What are you doing today to achieve that goal?"**

This is the question that separates intention from commitment. The answer places the candidate in the process.

| Stage | What you hear | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Not started | "I plan to", "someday", "once things settle down" | The goal is a wish. Unlikely to be serious |
| Planning | Has researched, decided, or scheduled a first step | Serious intent, unproven follow-through |
| Acting | Specific actions under way, with dates | Committed |
| Progressing | Measurable progress, setbacks named, adjustments made | Proven self-direction; the strongest signal in the interview |

**Follow-ups, one or two only:**

- "When did you start?"
- "What has it cost you so far: time, money, something you gave up?"
- "What is the next step, and when?"

### Scoring

Score both questions together on the 1 to 4 scale.

| Score | Pattern |
|---|---|
| 4 | A goal beyond the role, a real motivator shared, and measurable progress already made |
| 3 | A clear goal and specific actions under way |
| 2 | A clear goal, nothing started; or action toward a goal that is only about the role |
| 1 | No goal beyond the job, or goals that change when asked twice |

### Reading the combination

| | Has started | Has not started |
|---|---|---|
| **Big goal, personal and professional** | Self-driven builder. Highest signal | Talker. Ambition without follow-through; check references for the same pattern at work |
| **Goal limited to the role** | Steady executor. Reliable, may need external motivation | Drifter. Low signal for any Type 1 role |

A candidate who has not started on their own goal is unlikely to start on yours without close management. That is the point of the second question.

### Guardrails

These questions invite personal answers. Use them to read motivation and follow-through, never the content of someone's private life.

- Score the pattern (horizon, motivation, stage of progress), never what the goal is.
- Never follow up on, record, or weigh family plans, relationships, health, religion, age, or any other protected topic, even if the candidate raises it. Note "personal goal, in progress" and move on.
- Do not penalize a candidate who keeps the answer professional. Private people and different cultures share differently. Treat a role-only answer as neutral and probe motivation another way.
- Ask every candidate for the role the same two questions, worded the same way.
- Check local employment law before using personal-life questions in your jurisdiction.
