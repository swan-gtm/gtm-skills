Applies to any team with at least one role the business cannot afford to lose. Produces a bench scorecard, a performance and potential map, succession plans, and a talent review outcome.

**The doctrine:** succession asks who can do the next job. Bench strength proves it before you need them. A résumé only proves what someone did in the last system. A succession plan is a development plan with names attached, built years before the vacancy, and potential is a claim that needs evidence.

## The sequence

1. **Name the critical roles and scan the horizon.** A role is critical if losing its holder would put a quarter's number, a top customer, or a key capability at risk within 90 days. Include the leader's own role. Then scan 18 to 24 months ahead: retirements, flight risk, and promotions that will open seats. Read `references/bench-scorecard.md`.
2. **Score the bench.** For each critical role: how many qualified backfills, how soon each is ready, and whether the incumbent is a single point of failure. Compute the aggregate bench score.
3. **Assess performance with evidence.** Results against target, quality of results, and how they were produced. No placement without data.
4. **Assess potential with evidence.** Four signals: learning speed, aspiration, the altitude the person already operates at, and whether others get better around them. Read `references/potential-and-readiness.md`.
5. **Map and decide readiness.** Place people on the performance and potential grid as a conversation tool, then make a readiness call for each successor: ready now, ready in 12 months, ready in 24 months, or not a successor for this role.
6. **Run the talent review.** Independent pre-scoring, evidence on the table, bias checks, and decisions with owners. Read `references/talent-review.md`.
7. **Write the plans.** For each critical role: interim cover, short-term backfill, permanent successor and development plan, and the trigger for an external hire. Build readiness through real cross-functional work, not classroom development. When the plan calls for an outside hire, raise the evidence standard: test whether past success transfers to this operating system. Read `references/succession-plans.md`.
8. **Review quarterly.** Re-score the bench, update readiness, and measure in three tiers: actions taken, coverage and readiness moving, and outcomes when transitions happen.

## What good looks like

- **The expert's tell:** every critical role has a named, evidence-backed successor or an explicit external-hire trigger, and the leader's own role is on the list.
- **The common mistake:** the high-potential list is a popularity list. Polish, visibility, and senior sponsorship stand in for evidence of learning speed and aspiration.
- **The second mistake:** naming successors without developing them. A name on a slide with no stretch assignment is not a bench.
- **The external-hire mistake:** reading title and scale as readiness. The most dangerous succession mistakes are outstanding candidates evaluated against the wrong evidence.
- **The third mistake:** ignoring people who are outgrowing their roles. Top performers with no credible path become someone else's bench.
- **How to know it worked:** when a critical role opens, a ready successor steps in, performance holds through the transition, and regrettable attrition among top performers stays low.

## Rules

- MUST include the leader's own role in the critical-role list.
- MUST back every performance and potential placement with evidence; NEVER place people on impressions.
- MUST assess potential on learning speed, aspiration, operating altitude, and multiplier evidence; NEVER on polish, tenure, or visibility.
- MUST collect independent ratings before the talent review discussion.
- MUST name an interim cover and an external-hire trigger for every critical role without a ready-now successor.
- NEVER mark an external candidate ready now from title and scale alone. Require comparable work, operating-context fit, adaptability, and multiplier fit.
- MUST give every named successor a development action with a date.
- NEVER treat a grid placement as permanent. Recalibrate every quarter.
- NEVER use protected characteristics, or proxies for them, in any placement.
- MUST flag top performers outgrowing their roles and name their next challenge or its timing.
