# Content de-slop QA

Applies to every AI-assisted draft before publishing or sending. Produces the same copy with the machine tells removed and the vague claims made concrete.

Run four passes in order: structure, surface, specificity, rhythm. The order is the method. Reversing it produces a draft that has been word-swapped and still reads as machine output.

## 1. Fix structure before words

Structural tells expose a draft even after every suspicious word is swapped. Scan for these first and rewrite the sentence, never just the word.

- **The contrast flip.** "This isn't X. It's Y." Also every cousin: "Not just X, but Y." "X? Think again." Rewrite as a direct claim about Y.
- **Triads.** Three parallel items in list after list, sentence after sentence. Cut to two or extend to four.
- **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence the same length, every paragraph the same depth. Humans run long and then fragment.
- **The summary close.** "The bottom line?" "Here's the thing." And its mirror, the rhetorical question opener.
- **Over-signposting.** Announcing what the piece will say before saying it.

Each of these has a standard rewrite. The full inventory, with the rewrite pattern for every entry, is in `references/tell-inventory.md`.

## 2. Then the surface tells

Once the skeleton is human, clean the surface:

- Punctuation the author never uses, starting with em dashes.
- Written-out forms where a person contracts: it is, do not, cannot.
- Aphoristic closers reaching for profundity.
- Hedging stacks: "arguably one of the most."
- Bold on every other line, emoji bullets, hashtag piles.
- Superlatives no one could verify.

This pass is cheap and it is not the work. A draft that has only had this pass run on it still reads as generated.

## 3. The specificity pass

Every generic claim either becomes a real number, name, or date pulled from source material, or it gets cut.

"Many teams struggle with reply rates" becomes the actual rate from the actual campaign, or nothing.

Where the source material lacks the specific, flag the gap for the author instead of inventing one. Fabricated specificity is worse slop than vagueness, because it survives editing and ships as a claim the company cannot support. Trace every number and name back to a source before it stays in.

Target at least one verifiable specific per section. Sections that cannot carry one are usually sections with nothing to say, and cutting them improves the piece.

## 4. The rhythm pass

Read the piece aloud. Then:

- Vary sentence length on purpose.
- Leave one fragment.
- Break at least one parallel structure per section.

The goal is copy that reads like one person typed it in one sitting.

## The fingerprint outranks the tell list

If a voice fingerprint document exists for the author, load it first and let it win every conflict.

Some humans genuinely open with rhetorical questions. Some lean on parallelism. Sanding off an author's real habits produces a second kind of fake, and their audience catches it just as fast as the first kind. The tell list describes the average machine draft. The fingerprint describes this person. When they disagree, the fingerprint is right.

Building a fingerprint from an author's genuine samples is covered in `references/voice-fingerprint.md`. Channel changes what to relax and what to tighten, and a cold email tolerates far less than a blog post: see `references/channel-variants.md`.

## What good looks like

The best operator reads for skeleton first: flips, triads, uniform rhythm. The mediocre version swaps "delve," deletes the em dashes, and ships, leaving the skeleton intact. The skeleton is the tell.

The test: paste the draft next to three genuine pieces by the same author. A reader who knows them picks the fake at no better than chance, or the pass failed.

Good output has uneven rhythm, at least one verifiable specific per section, nothing left from the tell inventory, and the author's actual quirks intact.

Worked before-and-after passes, including a case where a flagged construction was correct and stayed, are in `references/worked-examples.md`.

## Rules

- MUST rewrite structure before touching word choice.
- MUST trace every number and name to source material, or cut it.
- MUST load the author's voice fingerprint before applying the tell list, when one exists.
- NEVER compensate with slang, forced edge, or manufactured casualness.
- NEVER treat paraphrase alone as humanizing. It preserves the structure that gives the draft away.
- NEVER invent a specific to fill a gap. Flag the gap.
