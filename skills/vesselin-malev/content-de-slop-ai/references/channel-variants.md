# Channel variants

The four passes are constant. What changes by channel is the tolerance for each tell and where the specificity has to land.

Ordered from least tolerant to most.

## Cold email and sequence steps

The least tolerant channel. A recipient who has no relationship with the sender is actively looking for a reason to delete, and a single machine tell supplies it.

- **Structure:** no signposting at all, no summary close, no triads. Three sentences is a long cold email.
- **Surface:** match the sender's actual punctuation, including their real habits in Sent Mail. Contractions always.
- **Specificity:** the traceable observation goes in the first line, not the second. If the first line could be sent to any account on the list, the email fails regardless of what follows.
- **Extra rule:** the claim has to be bounded. "Two other teams fixed this in about a week" survives scrutiny. "Drive measurable pipeline growth" does not.

Personalization tokens are a separate failure mode. A merge field that resolves to a company name is not a specific.

## Social posts

- **Structure:** the contrast flip is most damaging here, because the format rewards openers and readers have seen thousands of them. Triads in bullet form are the second tell.
- **Surface:** emoji bullets, hashtag piles, and bold-per-line are platform slop independent of AI. Cut them unless the fingerprint shows the author uses them.
- **Specificity:** one number in the first two lines, or the post reads as generic. The number is what stops the scroll, not the hook.
- **Rhythm:** short lines are native to the format, which makes uniform rhythm harder to spot. Measure anyway. Fifteen consecutive one-line paragraphs is its own uniformity.

## Landing pages and product copy

- **Structure:** the contrast flip hides in headlines. "Not another CRM." Rewrite as the direct claim.
- **Surface:** superlatives and unverifiable claims carry legal and trust risk here, not just credibility risk. Cut anything the company could not support if asked.
- **Specificity:** every proof element needs a source. A logo, a named customer, a number with a date attached.
- **Relax:** parallel structure across feature sections is design, not slop. Three feature cards with parallel headings are fine.

## Newsletters

- **Structure:** the summary close is the most common tell, because the format invites a wrap-up. End on the last real point.
- **Surface:** the author's voice matters more than in any other channel, since subscribers opted in to a person. The fingerprint outranks everything.
- **Specificity:** at least one specific per section, and the sections are usually short.
- **Relax:** over-signposting is partly acceptable when the newsletter has a recurring structure readers navigate by. A standing section header is not signposting.

## Long-form blog and documentation

The most tolerant channel, and the one where uniform rhythm does the most damage over length.

- **Structure:** check triads across the whole piece, not per section. Long-form is where three lists of three accumulate unnoticed.
- **Surface:** the register vocabulary list matters most here, because length gives it room to compound.
- **Specificity:** one verifiable specific per section is a floor, not a target. Sections that cannot carry one should be cut or merged.
- **Relax:** signposting is legitimate in documentation. A reader scanning for a procedure benefits from knowing what a section covers.

## Sales collateral and decks

- **Structure:** triads are structural to the format. Three columns, three bullets, three phases. Judge the copy inside the structure, not the structure.
- **Specificity:** every number needs a date and a source, because these documents get forwarded and outlive the campaign that produced them.
- **Extra rule:** no undated claim survives. "40% faster" without a date and a source becomes a liability the moment a prospect asks.
