# Channel calibration

The four passes are constant. What changes by channel is which tells do the most damage and where the specificity has to land.

## Feed posts

The hook carries everything. Line one must make the subject unmistakable, because confusion equals scroll and the feed truncates early.

AI drafts fail hooks two ways: the rhetorical question that hides the subject, and the clever-but-vague opener that could introduce any topic. Put the subject and the tension in line one, in plain words.

- **Hashtag piles read as automation** and suppress reach past the first few. Use none or almost none.
- **The bolted-on question** appended to force comments, "What do you think?" or "Agree?", now reduces reach rather than raising it. If the post deserves discussion, the discussion prompt is the post's actual stance.
- **The staircase format** became the machine's default post shape and pattern-matches as AI on sight. Write real paragraphs with one earned single-line break.
- **Distribution follows dwell time, saves, and long comments,** and generic content earns none of the three. Saves are worth several likes each. Target one save-worthy specific per post: a number, a template, a named mechanism the reader wants to keep.
- **Feeds reward accounts that stay in lane.** Injecting specifics from the author's actual territory doubles as a topic-consistency pass.
- **Batch signatures.** Posting the same structure at the same time across multiple accounts installs a shared pattern the feed prices in. Vary structure, timing, and format, and watch per-account baselines for correlated movement.

## Cold email

The reader's question is always the same: did a human write this to me, specifically? Template smell answers it before the pitch begins.

- **Fake personalization is worse than none.** A scraped first-line compliment, "Loved your recent post!", flags automation harder than a plain opener. Personalize with something that required actually reading: a specific claim they made, a number from their site, a hiring signal interpreted.
- **Kill the clichés on sight:** "Hope this finds you well," "Just following up," "quick question" subject lines, "I know you're busy." Each one is a template fingerprint.
- **One CTA, low friction.** Multiple asks read as a sequence step, because they are one.
- **Brevity is a human signal.** Machines pad. Operators respect the reader's ten seconds. Under 100 words usually beats over 150.
- **Deliverability compounds the problem.** Pattern-matched template copy at volume burns domain reputation alongside reply rates. Varying real specifics per recipient protects both.

## Newsletters and long-form

Register mismatch is the dominant tell here. The punchy one-liner style that works in a feed post reads as a failure in an essay. Long-form demands narrative, stakes, and development, and AI defaults produce neither. A long piece assembled from feed-post rhythms is slop wearing a word count.

- **Earn the length.** Every section must advance an argument or a story. The listicle skeleton with headers restating each other is the long-form staircase.
- **Personal narrative is the moat:** real chronology, real decisions, real cost. A model cannot have been there, which is exactly why being there is the differentiator.
- **Keep the piece pointed at the reader's situation.** Newsletter copy lives on "you." Slop drifts into third-person generalities about "businesses" and "teams."
- **The intro that summarizes and the conclusion that restates** are both machine defaults. Open inside the story; end on the sharpest specific.
- **Signposting is legitimate in documentation.** A reader scanning for a procedure benefits from knowing what a section covers. This is the one channel where that relaxes.

## Case studies and proof content

The de-AI pass here is an evidence pass, because models fabricate by default when the source is thin.

- **Every number traces to source material or gets cut.** No composite anecdotes, no rounded-up results, no invented timelines.
- **Run the defensible-claim test on every superlative.** Would it survive a hostile reader with a search bar? Weaken claims until they are defensible. The piece gains credibility with every downgrade.
- **Anchors beat adjectives.** One hard number with context outperforms a paragraph of "significant improvements."
- **Dates attach to every claim.** These documents get forwarded and outlive the campaign that produced them. An undated "40% faster" becomes a liability the moment a prospect asks.
- **In regulated industries,** healthcare, finance, and legal, a fabricated claim is a compliance incident, and this pass is non-negotiable.

## Sales collateral and decks

- **Triads are structural to the format.** Three columns, three bullets, three phases. Judge the copy inside the structure, not the structure.
- **Every number needs a date and a source,** for the same forwarding reason as proof content.
- **Superlatives carry legal risk here,** not just credibility risk. Cut anything the company could not support if asked.
